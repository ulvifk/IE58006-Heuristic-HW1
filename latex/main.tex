\section*{Part C: Computational Experiments}

\subsection*{C1: Effect of the Cooling Factor $\alpha$}

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \input{tables/c1_cooling_rate}
    \caption{C1: effect of the cooling factor $\alpha$ over seeds 0--9 ($T_0 = 3.0$, $T_{min} = 0.02$, cooling every 20 candidate moves).}
    \label{tab:c1-cooling-rate}
\end{table}

\paragraph{C1(d): trade-off.}
As Table~\ref{tab:c1-cooling-rate} shows, slower cooling (a larger $\alpha$) lengthens the run, because the temperature needs more cooling steps to reach $T_{min}$: the number of candidate moves grows from 460 at $\alpha = 0.80$ to 9980 at $\alpha = 0.99$. Since the temperature stays high for longer, the search accepts far more worse moves. This stronger emphasis on exploration lets the search escape poor regions before it settles, which improves the final tours: the mean best length drops from 44.82 to 41.15. With many more iterations, the result also depends less on the seed, and the standard deviation shrinks from 2.55 to 0.61. The cost is computational effort, which grows in proportion to the number of candidate moves.

\subsection*{C2: Effect of the Initial Temperature $T_0$}

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \input{tables/c2_initial_temperature}
    \caption{C2: effect of the initial temperature $T_0$ over seeds 0--9 ($\alpha = 0.90$, $T_{min} = 0.02$, cooling every 20 candidate moves).}
    \label{tab:c2-initial-temperature}
\end{table}

\paragraph{C2: interpretation.}
As Table~\ref{tab:c2-initial-temperature} shows, the initial temperature mainly controls how freely the search moves early on. At $T_0 = 1.0$ the run starts almost greedy: only 1.74\% of the worse moves are accepted, so the search settles into the first good region it finds, which gives the worst mean (43.55) and the largest standard deviation (2.09). At $T_0 = 6.0$ the share of accepted worse moves rises to 9.15\%, and the first cooling steps are spent wandering almost at random. This makes the results more consistent (standard deviation 0.95) but does not improve the mean (42.89). $T_0 = 3.0$ strikes the best balance and gives the best mean (42.62). The computational effort grows only slowly with $T_0$. The run grows from 760 to 1100 candidate moves.

\subsection*{C3: Recommended Configuration}

\paragraph{C3(a): choice.}
I choose $T_0 = 6.0$ and $\alpha = 0.99$, because robustness matters more than one good run. In C2, $T_0 = 6.0$ has a slightly worse mean than $T_0 = 3.0$ (42.89 against 42.62), but a much lower standard deviation (0.95 against 1.40), and it adds only a few candidate moves (1100 against 960). Likewise in C1, $\alpha = 0.99$ keeps the temperature high for longer, so the search explores more and is more likely to reach a high-quality solution in every run, which gives the lowest standard deviation (0.61).

\paragraph{C3(c): comparison.}
Compared with the baseline settings, the recommended configuration lowers the mean from 42.62 to 40.96 and the standard deviation from 1.40 to 0.42. The price is about twelve times as many candidate moves (11360 against 960). Tuning each parameter on its own, as in C1 and C2, is not ideal, because the parameters are not independent: they affect each other. If time allows, it is better to test all combinations. The instance size also matters. My configuration needs much more computation than the baseline, and whether that is acceptable depends on the solve time. Since this instance is very small, a twelvefold increase in solve time changes nothing in practice.

\section*{Part D: Interpretation and Reflection}

\paragraph{(a)}
A worse move with $\Delta > 0$ is accepted with probability
\[
p = e^{-\Delta / T}.
\]
For a fixed $\Delta$, a higher temperature gives a higher $p$, so some random worsening moves are applied and the search drifts away from the local optimum it is in. This is exploration, or diversification. In C2, 9.15\% of the worse moves were accepted at $T_0 = 6.0$ but only 1.74\% at $T_0 = 1.0$. As $T$ goes to zero, $p$ goes to zero as well, so only improving moves are accepted and the search behaves like greedy local search.

\paragraph{(b)}
Since worsening moves can be accepted, the current solution at any point of the run, including its end, may not be the best solution visited so far. In Table~\ref{tab:first-moves}, the current length rises to 84.72 at iteration 7 while the best-so-far stays at 76.83. Without a separate copy of the best-so-far, that solution would be lost, and the run would report whatever the current solution happened to be when it stopped.

\paragraph{(c)}
In the baseline run (seed 7), iteration 7 in Table~\ref{tab:first-moves} accepts a worsening move with $\Delta = +7.25$ at $T = 3.0$, since $u = 0.0832 < p = 0.0892$, and the current length rises from 77.47 to 84.72. From this new position the search finds improving moves again, and at iteration 11 it reaches a new best-so-far of 76.14, better than the 76.83 it had before the worsening move.
